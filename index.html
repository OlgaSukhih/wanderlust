The story

Maya had always suffered from wanderlust, but this time she promised herself she would take a perfectly ordinary two-week holiday in Lisbon. On her third evening, however, she met an elderly photographer called Elias who claimed that his backpack had been abducted — his word, not hers — by a monkey during a trip years earlier. Maya laughed, assuming he was trying to feign seriousness, but Elias produced a photograph of the animal disappearing across a roof with the bag.

The strange travel stories began to pile up. A missed ferry. A night in a desert village. A passport found inside a loaf of bread. By midnight, one idea had embedded itself in Maya's mind: perhaps the best journeys were the ones that went wrong. The next morning, instead of following her itinerary, she bought a ticket to Tangier.

At the port, a man tried to sell her an absurdly expensive “official” tour. She managed to dodge him by pretending to receive an urgent phone call. Then her bank blocked her card after the unexpected border crossing, so she had to withdraw cash from the only ATM she could find. Standing there with no hotel booking, no working card and almost no plan, Maya realised she was happier than she had been in months.
